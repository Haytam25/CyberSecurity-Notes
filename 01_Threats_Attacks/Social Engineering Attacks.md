## Definition
> Social engineering is the psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information. It often exploits human trust and curiosity.

---

## 🎯 Common Social Engineering Techniques

## 1. **Phishing**
- Fraudulent emails or websites that impersonate legitimate sources to steal data.
- Often includes links or attachments.

#### Subtypes:
- **Spear Phishing**: Targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific individual or organization.
- **Whaling**: Aimed at high-level executives.
- **Smishing**: SMS-based phishing.
- **Vishing**: Voice call-based phishing.

---

### 2. **Pretexting**
- Attacker creates a fabricated scenario to trick the victim into providing info.
- Example: Pretending to be IT support needing login credentials.

---

### 3. **Baiting**
- Enticing a victim with something appealing (e.g., free software, USB drive).
- Goal: Trick the victim into running malware or providing data.

---

### 4. **Tailgating (Piggybacking)**
- Gaining physical access to restricted areas by following an authorized person.

---

### 5. **Impersonation**
- Attacker pretends to be someone else (e.g., a trusted colleague or boss) to get access or information.

---

### 6. **Dumpster Diving**
- Looking through physical trash for information (e.g., discarded papers with passwords or PII).

---

### 7. **Quid Pro Quo**
- Attacker offers something (e.g., help or reward) in exchange for sensitive information.

---

## 🛡️ Prevention Techniques
- Security awareness training
- Anti-phishing filters
- Two-factor authentication
- Verification of identities
- Clean desk policy
- Shred documents before disposal
